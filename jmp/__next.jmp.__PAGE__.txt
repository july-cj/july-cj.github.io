2:T470,Community colleges enroll millions of low-income, working students who are uniquely exposed to local economic conditions. Despite evidence that aggregate CC enrollment is countercyclical, we lack causal evidence on how recessions affect students already enrolled. Using longitudinal administrative data from Minnesota (first-time CC enrollees, 2005-2012) linked to Unemployment Insurance wage records, this study employs a Bartik shift-share instrument embedded in a difference-in-differences design to identify the effects of local employment downturns on persistence, work behavior, and credential completion. Wild cluster bootstrap inference and Bonferroni correction address small cluster and multiple comparison concerns; event-study tests support parallel pre-trends. Preliminary results reveal substantial heterogeneity: recession exposure has no significant average effect, but Pell-eligible students and recently employed students persist more while full-time starters are significantly less likely to persist. Findings demonstrate how external economic shocks reshape trajectories for financially vulnerable, working learners.7:X
